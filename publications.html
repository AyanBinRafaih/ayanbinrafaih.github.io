  • Detecting False Positives with Derived Planetary Parameters: Experimenting with the KEPLER Dataset

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Zachary Murray

    Open Journal of Astrophysics 2025

    arXiv

    Most ML pipelines for exoplanet classification rely on full time-series light curves, which are computationally expensive and GPU-dependent. We test whether a small set of derived planetary parameters such as transit depth, duration, stellar radius and odd-even depth comparison, can do the same job. Across four classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Machines, Convolutional Neural Networks) on the KEPLER dataset, the derived-parameter approach identifies roughly 90% of false positives, with Random Forest and CNN giving the best accuracy-precision tradeoff (87.8% and 92.2% respectively). The stellar eclipse flag proves easiest to classify; ephemeris-match and centroid-offset flags are hardest. This allows for the usage of lightweight classification pipelines for astrophysical data.

  • Sustainability in Surgery: Problems, Solutions, and Recommendations

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Kaso Ari

    Cureus 2025

    PDF

    Healthcare generates a significant carbon footprint, and operating rooms are a disproportionate contributor through single-use devices, pharmaceutical waste, and high energy consumption. We review the sources of surgical waste and survey the evidence for mitigation strategies, including waste-reduction protocols, energy-efficient equipment, sustainable procurement, and circular-economy approaches. We close with practical recommendations for surgical teams, administrators, and policymakers, framing sustainability not just as an environmental obligation but as a lever for cost savings and improved care delivery, especially in modern surgical procedures. 

    Rafaih AB, Ari K. Sustainability in Surgery: Problems, Solutions, and Recommendations. Cureus. 2025;17(10):e94010. Published 2025 Oct 7. doi:10.7759/cureus.94010

  • Comparing Surgical Outcomes of Robotic and Laparoscopic or Open Ileal Pouch–Anal Anastomosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Abdelrahman Abdelaal, Kaso Ari, Ayan Bin Rafaih, Ayaz Ahmed Memon, Irshad Shaikh

    Journal of Robotic Surgery 2025

    PDF

    Ileal pouch–anal anastomosis (IPAA) is the definitive surgical option for ulcerative colitis and familial adenomatous polyposis requiring colectomy, but whether the robotic approach offers advantages over laparoscopic or open surgery is unclear. Our meta-analysis of 10 studies (3,166 patients) finds that robotic IPAA is associated with a significantly shorter hospital stay and a non-significant trend toward fewer conversions to open surgery. Operative times were longer with robotics; blood loss and postoperative complication rates were broadly comparable across all three approaches. 

    Abdelaal A, Ari K, Rafaih AB, Memon AA, Shaikh I, Iqbal MR. Comparing surgical outcomes of robotic and laparoscopic or open ileal pouch-anal anastomosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Robot Surg. 2025;19(1):544. Published 2025 Sep 1. doi:10.1007/s11701-025-02707-5

  • Robotic Beyond Total Mesorectal Excision (bTME) for Locally Advanced and Recurrent Anorectal Cancer — A Systematic Review

    Joachim Cheng En Ho, Aryan Raj Goel, Muriel Sirgi, Ayan Bin Rafaih, Ayaz Ahmed Memon, Irshad Shaikh

    Journal of Robotic Surgery 2025

    PDF

    Standard total mesorectal excision cannot achieve clear margins in locally advanced or recurrent rectal cancers and often these cases require a more extensive beyond-TME resection. We conduct a PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 19 studies across 1,027 patients to assess whether robotic bTME is a feasible alternative to open surgery. We find that despite longer operative times, robotic bTME is associated with reduced blood loss and acceptable morbidity; the 1-year survival rate exceeded 90% and the recurrence rate was 24.9%. Significant heterogeneity across studies, however, makes definitive conclusions difficult, and further prospective data with standardised outcomes are needed. 

    Ho, J.C.E., Goel, A.R., Sirgi, M. et al. Robotic Beyond Total Mesorectal Excision (bTME) for locally advanced and recurrent anorectal cancer: a systematic review. J Robotic Surg 19, 397 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-025-02573-1

  • Robotic Prosthetic Arm with 4 Degree of Freedom Rotational Movement

    Ayan Bin Rafaih

    International Research Journal of Engineering and Technology 2025

    PDF

    A low-cost, Arduino-driven 4-DOF prosthetic arm for post-amputation rehabilitation, using four servo motors for radial and lateral movement, Bluetooth (HC-05) control from a simple Android app, and a 3D-printed frame to keep manufacturing costs down. Bench tests show reliable gripping, lifting procedures and positioning within the arm's workspace.

  • Exploring the Roles of Extended Reality Technologies in Advancing Colorectal Surgical Training

    Dominic Araszkiewicz, Taner Shakir, Gita Lingam, Ayan Bin Rafaih, Manish Chand

    Frontline Gastroenterology 2025

    PDF

    Record NHS waiting lists and a shortage of trained endoscopists have created pressure for better surgical training tools. We review the evidence for augmented, virtual, and mixed reality in colorectal surgical training to evaluate their efficacy in NHS hospitals. VR has the strongest evidence base, particularly for endoscopy, laparoscopy and robotics simulation, while AR's strength lies in intraoperative image overlays and anatomical visualisation. Mixed reality is newer but expanding in this field. XR offers objective assessment and immediate feedback for pathology compared to conventional training; however, its main gaps are a lack of standardised protocols and limited coverage of patient communication skills. 

    Atraszkiewicz D, Shakir T, Lingam G, et alExploring the roles of extended reality technologies in advancing colorectal surgical trainingFrontline Gastroenterology 2025;16:495-502.

  • Gyro Sensor Based Smart Helmet for Automated Early Accident Detection

    Ayan Bin Rafaih

    International Research Journal of Engineering and Technology 2025

    PDF

    An Arduino-based helmet that uses a gyroscope to flag sudden, abnormal accelerations consistent with a crash. On detection, a GSM module sends an SMS alert, including GPS coordinates from a paired module to a pre-set emergency contact, giving riders an automatic early-warning layer on top of standard helmet safety.

  • Artificial Intelligence Driven Approaches to Managing Surgeon Fatigue and Improving Performance

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Kaso Ari

    Cureus 2024

    PDF

    Duty-hour limits and scheduled breaks address surgeon fatigue bluntly since they do not account for inter-individual variation or intraoperative deterioration in real time. We review how AI-based monitoring (HRV, EEG, and computer-vision behavioural analysis) could enable continuous, personalised fatigue detection and trigger targeted interventions such as dynamic break scheduling or task redistribution. The evidence base remains early-stage, and we find that adoption raises unresolved questions around data privacy, patient consent, and surgeon autonomy (in terms of decision-making) that any rule framework would need to address. 

    Rafaih A, Ari K (December 14, 2024) Artificial Intelligence-Driven Approaches to Managing Surgeon Fatigue and Improving Performance. Cureus 16(12): e75717. doi:10.7759/cureus.75717
