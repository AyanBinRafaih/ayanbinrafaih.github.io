About

Hello! I'm Ayan Bin Rafaih and have just graduated from Aitchison College. My interests and my work sit at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability in LLMs for reasoning capabilities and multi-agent environments, evaluating chain-of-thought reasoning traces in LLMs for complex problems and low-latency computational optimisations for trading systems and microservices.

Currently, I'm working at King's College on developing policy-informed steering methods in LLMs to see if there is a defined subset of internal steering parameters in the activation space for agentic behaviours, including cooperation and defection, that are transferable across different-policy environments and transplantable across various agentic instances, specifically in game-theoretic scenarios. I'm also, on the side, working at University of Washington's SyFI Lab on VibeSys (TL;DR using agentic loops to generate bespoke serving systems). I'm currently leading the development of the Terminal UI for this project (and we are adding very cool features!).

Recently, I have worked on benchmarking SOTA LLMs on olympiad problems, evaluating reasoning traces for complex problems in physics and astrophysics (using human annotators and LLM-as-a-Judge techniques), identifying conceptual pitfalls, logical fallacies and hallucination instances in model responses. I have also worked with Dr. Zachary Murray at Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics and Université Côte d'Azur on using simple, light-weight architectures for false positive detection using derived planetary parameters. Alongside that, I was at UCL Hawkes Institute working with Prof. Evangelos Mazomenos on robotic surgical skill classification using Bayesian-optimisation-based learning methods and acceleration-based kinematics features.

Previously, I won an honourable mention at the 2nd International Olympiad of Artificial Intelligence (IOAI 2025). I also won 2 distinguished honourable mention awards at the Stanford University Mathematics Tournament (SMT 2024, 2025), and earned 6 Outstanding Cambridge Learner Award (OCLA 2023, 2024, 2025) distinctions.

Outside of academics, I'm into kart-racing and hiking. I also enjoy playing tennis and listening to music.

Research interests: Mechanistic Interpretability · Agentic AI Systems · ML Systems · Reasoning Benchmarks · Large Language Models · AI4Science Low-Latency System Architecture · Computational Physics · Optimisation Techniques · Surgical Robotics
