  • Detecting False Positives with Derived Planetary Parameters: Experimenting with the KEPLER Dataset

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Zachary Murray

    The Open Journal of Astrophysics · 2025

    PDF arXiv Poster — add link

    Tests whether transit false-positive detection can rely on a handful of derived planetary parameters — stellar radius, transit depth and duration, odd–even depth comparison — instead of full light curves. Across four classifiers (logistic regression, random forest, SVM, CNN), this lighter feature set recovers roughly 90% of known false positives, with the random forest and CNN giving the strongest accuracy / precision–recall trade-off (87.8% and 92.2% validation accuracy respectively). Performance is also broken down by the Kepler vetting flags: the stellar-eclipse flag is easiest to classify, while centroid-offset and ephemeris-match flags are the hardest.

  • AstroThink: Can Large Language Models Reason Their Way Through Astrophysics Olympiad Problems?

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Taner Shakir

    Under review, Artificial Intelligence Review · accepted for oral presentation, ICMLC 2026

    Preprint — coming soon

    Olympiad benchmarks already exist for mathematics (IMO), informatics (IOI), and physics (IPhO), but astrophysics has comparatively little coverage beyond multiple-choice formats. We evaluate several state-of-the-art LLMs on the 2024 International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA) exam, scoring them with a combination of human and LLM judges plus a qualitative error taxonomy. No model reaches medal-level performance — the strongest, DeepSeek-V3.1, scores 44.83%. The models struggle most with multi-step reasoning, often applying irrelevant methods or misreading a problem's physical setup.

  • Sustainability in Surgery: Problems, Solutions, and Recommendations

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Kaso Ari

    Cureus · 2025

    PDF

    Surgical care is a notable contributor to healthcare's carbon footprint. This review traces that footprint to operating-room waste, energy use, pharmaceutical waste, and single-use devices, then surveys current mitigation strategies — from waste reduction and energy efficiency to sustainable procurement and circular-economy approaches. It closes with practical recommendations for administrators, surgical teams, and policymakers, framing sustainability as both an environmental obligation and a route to cost savings and better care delivery.

  • Comparing Surgical Outcomes of Robotic and Laparoscopic or Open Ileal Pouch–Anal Anastomosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Abdelrahman Abdelaal, Kaso Ari, Ayan Bin Rafaih, Ayaz Ahmed Memon, Irshad Shaikh

    Journal of Robotic Surgery · 2025

    PDF

    A systematic review and meta-analysis (10 studies, 3,166 patients) comparing robotic, laparoscopic, and open ileal pouch–anal anastomosis for ulcerative colitis and familial adenomatous polyposis. Robotic IPAA was associated with a shorter hospital stay and a non-significant trend toward fewer conversions to open surgery, but longer operative times; blood loss and postoperative complication rates were comparable across approaches.

  • Exploring the Roles of Extended Reality Technologies in Advancing Colorectal Surgical Training

    Dominic Araszkiewicz, Taner Shakir, Gita Lingam, Ayan Bin Rafaih, Manish Chand

    Frontline Gastroenterology · 2025

    PDF

    A review of augmented, virtual, and mixed reality's role in colorectal surgical training amid rising waiting lists and a shortage of trained endoscopists. AR supports simulation and intraoperative guidance through image overlays and 3D visualisation of anatomy; VR has the stronger evidence base, particularly for endoscopy, laparoscopy, and robotics simulation. The review also flags open questions around standardisation and patient-communication training that current XR tools don't address.

  • Artificial Intelligence Driven Approaches to Managing Surgeon Fatigue and Improving Performance

    Ayan Bin Rafaih, Kaso Ari

    Cureus · 2024

    PDF

    A review of how AI-driven monitoring — heart-rate variability, EEG, and computer-vision-based behavioural analysis — could support real-time fatigue detection in surgeons, where conventional measures like duty-hour limits and scheduled breaks fall short.
